    3 Understand the map

    HDB and private homes compared

    Means are weighted by dwelling units. "Within subzone" compares HDB and private homes only inside the 124 subzones that have both.

    How is the score calculated?

    1. Count what is within reach. For each building and each of the five domains (Food, Groceries, Healthcare, Parks & Recreation, Public Transport), the map counts the amenities reachable along walking routes within the selected time. The exits of one MRT station count as one station, and each park counts once.

    2. Put the domains on the same scale (normalisation). The domains have very different numbers of amenities: a building may reach 20 bus stops but only one supermarket. Each count is therefore compared with a fixed benchmark: the count that 95% of buildings stay at or below within 10 minutes. The benchmarks are Food 9, Groceries 7, Healthcare 23, Parks & Recreation 5 and Public Transport 30. A count at or above the benchmark scores 1; a count of half the benchmark scores 0.5.

    3. Combine the domains. The five domain scores are averaged using the weights set with the sliders (equal by default) and shown on a 0–100 scale.

    What 100 means. A building scores 100 when it reaches the benchmark in every domain. This is possible: 46 buildings score 100 within 10 minutes with equal weights.

    Comparing scores. The benchmark is fixed, so a score shows how much a building can reach, not its rank among other buildings. Because the same benchmark applies to both walking times and to any weights, a score changes only when what the building can reach, or the weights, change.

    What the score does not mean. A score of 70 does not mean that 70% of daily needs are met. The score measures access to amenities by distance only; it does not measure walking comfort, shade, safety, or the quality and price of the amenities.
